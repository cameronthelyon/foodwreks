An OpenTable alternative with no cover fees

OpenTable charges a monthly fee plus a fee on every network cover, and it counts bookings from Google as network covers. {{BRAND}} costs $1,000 once and nothing per cover. The real question is how many of your guests actually come from OpenTable's app.

The Google detail

OpenTable bills reservations that come through Google at its network rate. A guest who searched your name on Google Maps, saw your Reserve button and booked is a diner who was already looking for you, and you pay a cover fee for them. With {{BRAND}}, your Google Business Profile reservation link points at your own booking page. Same button, no toll.

Where OpenTable is stronger

  • A diner network. OpenTable says 33 million diners use it each month. Some of them browse the app for somewhere to eat and will not find you elsewhere. {{BRAND}} has no marketplace app, on purpose.
  • Size. Integrations with almost everything, a large support team, and features for big groups and hotels.
  • Prepaid experiences and deposits. OpenTable sells them. {{BRAND}} holds cards for no-show fees but does not take prepayments yet.

Where {{BRAND}} is different

  • No toll on your own regulars. A cover costs nothing, wherever the guest found you.
  • Your guests stay yours. Export everything any day. No network shows your diners other restaurants.
  • Owned by restaurants. OpenTable belongs to Booking Holdings. {{BRAND}} is open source and being formed as a cooperative of the restaurants that use it. How that works
  • Your reports settle the argument. {{BRAND}} shows where every cover came from, so after a month you know what share was ever OpenTable's to charge for.

Who should stay, who should switch

Stay if a large share of your covers come from people browsing the OpenTable app who would not otherwise find you, or if you depend on prepaid experiences. Check your OpenTable cover report: it separates network, website and Google covers.

Switch if most of your guests find you on Google, Instagram, your website or by word of mouth, which is true for most neighborhood restaurants. You are paying a toll on diners who were already yours.
